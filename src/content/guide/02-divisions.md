The [field](/glossary/#field) is the background of the shield. It can be one flat tincture, and many of the best arms leave it at that. It can also be split into parts of different tinctures, and each way of splitting it has a name. A [division](/glossary/#division) is the cheapest way to make arms distinctive, because it needs no charges at all.

## Right is left

One warning before the names. The sides of a shield are named from the point of view of the person carrying it. [Dexter](/glossary/#dexter) is the bearer's right, which is the left as you look at the shield. [Sinister](/glossary/#sinister) is the bearer's left, which is your right. The top of the shield is the [chief](/glossary/#chief) and the bottom is the [base](/glossary/#base).

Dexter is the side of honour and comes first in every description. In practice you read a shield the way you read a page of English: from the top, and from your left.

## The divisions

Most divisions are named after the [ordinary](/glossary/#ordinary) that would run along the same line, with "per" in front. A pale is an upright band, so "per pale" means split upright, down the middle.

- [Per pale](/glossary/#per-pale): in two halves, side by side.
- [Per fess](/glossary/#per-fess): in two halves, top and bottom.
- [Per bend](/glossary/#per-bend): in two along the diagonal that falls from your top left.
- [Per bend sinister](/glossary/#per-bend-sinister): in two along the other diagonal.
- [Per chevron](/glossary/#per-chevron): in two by a line shaped like the rafters of a roof.
- [Per saltire](/glossary/#per-saltire): in four by both diagonals.
- [Quarterly](/glossary/#quarterly): in four by an upright line and a level one.

The blazon names the tinctures in reading order: the dexter half first for per pale, the upper part first for the rest. Quarterly and per saltire use two tinctures in turn, so "Quarterly argent and sable" is white at the top left and the bottom right.

```arms
Per pale argent and gules | Per pale
Per fess azure and Or | Per fess
Per bend Or and vert | Per bend
Per bend sinister argent and sable | Per bend sinister
Per chevron gules and argent | Per chevron
Per saltire azure and argent | Per saltire
Quarterly argent and sable | Quarterly: the arms of the house of Hohenzollern
```

A field can also be cut in three, which is called [tierced](/glossary/#tierced). It is more at home on the Continent than in Britain. The maker draws three kinds: tierced per pale (three upright strips), tierced per fess (three level strips) and tierced per pall (three parts that meet in a Y).

```arms
Tierced per pale azure, Or and vert | Tierced per pale
Tierced per pall gules, argent and azure | Tierced per pall
```

## Patterned fields

Carry on dividing and the field becomes a pattern of stripes or squares. These are named after ordinaries too:

- [Barry](/glossary/#barry): level stripes.
- [Paly](/glossary/#paly): upright stripes.
- [Bendy](/glossary/#bendy): diagonal stripes.
- [Chequy](/glossary/#chequy): a chequerboard.

A striped field has an even number of stripes, and the blazon says how many. "Barry of six argent and azure" is three white stripes and three blue, with white at the top. The even number matters. With an odd number, one tincture would hold both outer stripes, and the eye would read it as a plain field with bands lying on it. Heraldry blazons that differently: "Argent, three bars azure" has seven stripes, four white and three blue.

A pattern of a metal and a colour is safe ground for [the rule of tincture](/guide/tinctures/). The two tinctures lie side by side and neither is on top.

```arms
Barry of six argent and azure | Barry of six
Paly of six Or and gules | Paly of six
Bendy of six Or and azure, a bordure gules | Bendy of six within a red bordure: the old arms of the dukes of Burgundy
Chequy Or and azure | Chequy: the arms of the Warenne earls of Surrey
```

## Lines of partition

The line that splits a field does not have to be straight. Heraldry has a set of ornamental [lines of partition](/glossary/#line-of-partition), and the same lines can edge an ordinary. The maker draws four:

- [Engrailed](/glossary/#engrailed): a row of shallow scoops that meet in points.
- [Indented](/glossary/#indented): small saw teeth.
- [Wavy](/glossary/#wavy): gentle waves, the obvious choice for rivers, lakes and the sea.
- [Embattled](/glossary/#embattled): square teeth, like the top of a castle wall.

Many more exist. Invected is engrailed turned inside out, dancetty is a zigzag of a few large teeth, nebuly looks like clouds, and raguly looks like the stumps of lopped branches. They are on the maker's roadmap.

In a blazon the line comes straight after the division: "Per fess wavy argent and azure". A line changes the character of arms more than you might expect. A straight per fess of white and blue looks like a flag. The same field per fess wavy looks like a coast.

```arms
Per fess wavy argent and azure | Per fess wavy: sky over water
Per fess indented gules and argent | Per fess indented
Per pale embattled Or and sable | Per pale embattled
Per chevron engrailed gules and argent | Per chevron engrailed
```

In the maker, a line other than straight is offered for the divisions that have a single line: per pale, per fess, per bend, per bend sinister and per chevron.

## One coat or several

A quarterly field of two tinctures is one coat of arms. A shield that shows four different arms in its quarters is something else. That is [marshalling](/guide/marshalling/), where whole coats are combined on one shield, and it has rules of its own. The same goes for per pale. The maker draws divisions, and does not marshal arms yet.

## Using divisions in your own arms

A divided field does a lot of quiet work for an invented nation.

- It can stand for two things at once: two peoples, two provinces, land and sea. Vantmoor, a country of moor and marsh, might split its field per fess indented, green over gold.
- It gives you a second tincture without adding a charge. A field per pale with one bold charge across it is a complete design.
- It reads best when a metal meets a colour at the line. Two colours side by side are allowed, but the line between them fades at a distance.
- It makes relatives. A neighbouring state or a junior branch can keep the charge and change the division.

Pick a division in [the maker](/) and try each line on it. The blazon under the shield changes as you go, which is the quickest way to learn the names.
